# Security, Privacy, Legal, and Compliance Roles in Software Product Development

## Roles

- **Application Security Engineer / Product Security Engineer** — embedded or centralized engineers who run secure code review, tooling (SAST/DAST), and vulnerability triage for a product line. Organizational choice, not statutory. Distinguished from Security Champions: "Security champions are embedded developers with security responsibilities, while security engineers are dedicated security professionals." [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/academy/cloud-careers/what-is-a-security-champion)
- **Security Architect** — designs security controls and threat models at a system level; feeds into Microsoft SDL's Design phase ("Creating threat models; identifying potential security vulnerabilities and designing mitigations"). [Microsoft SDL](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/securityengineering/sdl/), [SecurityJourney](https://www.securityjourney.com/post/security-development-lifecycle-sdl-definition-best-practices)
- **Security Champion** — a developer on a feature team given extra security responsibility: "developers who take on additional responsibility as the security point of contact for their team. They review code for common security issues, advocate for secure design decisions, triage security findings, and serve as the bridge between the central security team and the development organisation." [OWASP Developer Guide](https://devguide.owasp.org/en/08-culture-process/02-security-champions/01-security-champions-program/), [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/academy/cloud-careers/what-is-a-security-champion). Organizational choice, used to scale a small central AppSec team.
- **Privacy Engineer** — implements privacy-by-design controls (data minimization, anonymization, access controls) in the product; organizational choice, no statutory basis, though its output often feeds a DPIA.
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** — the one **statutory** role in this list. Under GDPR Article 37, mandatory for: (a) public authorities/bodies, (b) organizations whose core activities require large-scale, regular, systematic monitoring of individuals, (c) organizations whose core activities involve large-scale processing of special-category or criminal-offence data. Must have "expert knowledge of data protection law and practices" (Art. 37(5)), can be employee or external contractor (Art. 37(6)), and per Article 39 must inform/advise on GDPR obligations, monitor compliance, advise on DPIAs, and act as contact point for the supervisory authority. Failure to appoint one when required can draw fines up to €10M or 2% of global turnover. [Secure Privacy](https://support.secureprivacy.ai/article/dpo-roles-and-responsibilities-under-gdpr/), [Secure Privacy Art.37 guide](https://support.secureprivacy.ai/article/when-is-appointing-a-dpo-mandatory/), [GDPR.info Art.37](https://gdprinfo.eu/gdpr-article-37-explained-when-and-why-a-data-protection-officer-is-mandatory)
- **Compliance Manager** — owns the overall control framework (SOC 2, ISO 27001, etc.), evidence collection, and audit scheduling; organizational choice driven by customer/market demand for certification, not by statute. [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/iso-27001-evidence-requirements), [ISMS.online](https://www.isms.online/soc-2/audit-prep/how-to-collect-audit-ready-evidence/)
- **Technical Program Manager for compliance** — coordinates cross-functional gates (security review, DPIA, legal sign-off) against a launch timeline; organizational choice, not found in any statute — a project-management role invented to make statutory/contractual gates actually execute on schedule. [unsourced — no single canonical source defines this title; role inferred from convergence of SDL/audit-cycle process descriptions]
- **IP / patent counsel** — a licensed attorney role; required to render a legally binding freedom-to-operate (FTO) opinion. Distinguished from research: "Prior art studies determine whether an invention can be patented … FTO studies evaluate whether a product or process can be exploited without infringing existing rights." [Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/types-patent-searches-prior-art-and-freedom-operate/), [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/when-is-a-freedom-to-operate-opinion-cost-effective.html)
- **Open-source license reviewer** — organizational choice (often folded into legal or a dedicated OSS-compliance function), producing the license compliance report described below; not statutory but often contractually required by customers or driven indirectly by copyright law compliance for OSS licenses.

**Statutory vs. organizational summary**: Only the **DPO** (GDPR Art. 37) is a legally mandated role with a named statutory basis. The DPIA itself (Art. 35) is a legally mandated *process gate*, not a role. FTO opinions require a licensed attorney to be legally binding, but "running FTO research" itself is not a licensed-role requirement — only the opinion is. Everything else (AppSec engineer, Security Architect, Security Champion, Privacy Engineer, Compliance Manager, TPM-for-compliance, in-house OSS license reviewer) is an organizational design choice.

## Responsibility boundaries

- **Central security team vs. Security Champion**: champions are "the first point of contact between developers and a central security team," triaging and escalating rather than replacing the central AppSec function. [OWASP Developer Guide](https://devguide.owasp.org/en/08-culture-process/02-security-champions/01-security-champions-program/)
- **SAMM vs. ASVS**: "OWASP SAMM is a maturity model for assessing and improving AppSec programs over time … OWASP ASVS is a detailed set of security controls that can be used for technical assessments, especially by developers and testers." SAMM answers "how mature is our program," ASVS answers "does this specific application meet a defined bar." [GlobalDots](https://www.globaldots.com/resources/blog/application-security-frameworks/), [Codific ASVS](https://codific.com/owasp-asvs-a-comprehensive-overview/)
- **BSIMM vs. SAMM/ASVS**: BSIMM is descriptive, not prescriptive — "a reflection of current practices observed in real-world software security programs," based on data from 111 participating organizations (BSIMM16), organized into 12 practices across Governance, Intelligence, SSDL Touchpoints, and Deployment domains. It tells you what peer organizations do, not what you should do. [Secureframe](https://secureframe.com/frameworks-glossary/bsimm), [Black Duck BSIMM16](https://www.blackduck.com/resources/analyst-reports/bsimm.html)
- **Research vs. legal advice boundary**: prior-art/FTO searching is legitimate internal research, but the *opinion* that a product is clear to ship is a legal opinion requiring counsel — "An FTO study is typically conducted at more advanced stages of a product or process's development, but ideally before its commercial launch," and cost runs "$3,000 … up to $30,000 and beyond" for a professional search. [UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/freedom-to-operate), [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/arndt-freedom-to-operate-opinions)
- **DPO independence**: the DPO must have "a direct reporting line to the highest level of management" and statutory independence protections — this is a boundary the GDPR itself draws, distinguishing the DPO from an ordinary compliance manager who reports within a normal management chain. [Secure Privacy](https://support.secureprivacy.ai/article/dpo-roles-and-responsibilities-under-gdpr/)

## Artifacts produced

- **Threat model document** — output of the SDL Design phase; enumerates STRIDE-categorized threats and mitigations for a system. Consumed by architects, developers (for mitigation implementation), and security reviewers at the Verification gate. [Microsoft SDL](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/securityengineering/sdl/)
- **DPIA / privacy review** — required by GDPR Art. 35 to contain "a systematic description of the envisaged processing operations and purposes, an assessment of necessity and proportionality, an assessment of risks to data subjects' rights and freedoms, and the measures envisaged to address the risks," carried out "prior to the processing." Consumed by the DPO (who must be consulted per Art. 39), the supervisory authority (on request), and engineering (to implement mitigations). [GDPR.eu](https://gdpr.eu/data-protection-impact-assessment-template/), [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/article-35-gdpr/)
- **Security review report** — internal AppSec sign-off document, typically scored against ASVS controls; consumed by engineering leadership and the launch-approval gate.
- **Penetration test report** — third-party or internal offensive-test findings; commonly required as part of SOC 2 / ISO 27001 evidence packages: "Common audit-ready evidence packages include the current ISO 27001 certificate, recent SOC 2 report, penetration test results, vulnerability scan output…" Consumed by auditors, customers (as due-diligence evidence), and engineering (as a remediation backlog). [ISMS.online](https://www.isms.online/soc-2/audit-prep/how-to-collect-audit-ready-evidence/)
- **SBOM (Software Bill of Materials)** — "a machine-readable inventory of all components — libraries, packages, modules, and dependencies," mandated for federal software suppliers under EO 14028 Section 10(j). Consumed by procurement/government customers, security teams (for vulnerability correlation), and increasingly commercial customers. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-order-14028-improving-nations-cybersecurity/software-security-supply-chains-software-1), [Anchore](https://anchore.com/sbom/latest-cybersecurity-executive-order-requires-an-sbom/)
- **License compliance report** — inventory of open-source dependencies and their license obligations; consumed by legal/OSS reviewers before a dependency is approved and by customers during due diligence. [unsourced — no single canonical definition found in this pass; inferred from license-review role and SBOM parallel]
- **Audit evidence package** — the aggregate of access logs, policy documents, pen-test results, and control-mapping records assembled for SOC 2 Type II (a 3–12 month observation window) or ISO 27001 (3-year certification cycle with surveillance audits in years two and three). Consumed by external auditors and, post-certification, by customers. [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/soc-2-evidence-collection-templates), [ISMS.online](https://www.isms.online/soc-2/audit-prep/how-to-collect-audit-ready-evidence/)
- **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)** — contractual artifact between controller and processor required under GDPR whenever personal data is processed by a third party; consumed by legal, customers, and the DPO for compliance verification. [unsourced — not independently searched this pass; standard GDPR practice, confirm with primary Art. 28 text if higher confidence needed]
- **FTO / prior-art memo** — internal research memo assessing patent landscape risk before launch; distinct from a formal FTO legal opinion. "At some point prior to product launch, the design must be frozen, and FTO should be reviewed one last time." Consumed by product/engineering leadership and, if risk is found, escalated to patent counsel. [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/when-is-a-freedom-to-operate-opinion-cost-effective.html)
- **Risk register** — running log of identified security/privacy/legal/compliance risks with severity, owner, and status; consumed by TPM/compliance leads and executive risk committees for launch go/no-go decisions. [unsourced — standard GRC practice, not independently sourced this pass]

## Handoff points

1. **Engineering → security review**: formalized in SDL as the Verification phase gate — "completing checks and approvals to verify that code conforms to SDL and meets design requirements" — before Release. [Microsoft SDL](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/securityengineering/sdl/), [SecurityJourney](https://www.securityjourney.com/post/security-development-lifecycle-sdl-definition-best-practices)
2. **Security finding → engineering backlog**: Security Champions serve as the routing point, since they "triage security findings" and "serve as the bridge between the central security team and the development organisation," rather than findings going directly and unfiltered into a generic backlog. [OWASP Developer Guide](https://devguide.owasp.org/en/08-culture-process/02-security-champions/01-security-champions-program/)
3. **Late security findings are measurably expensive**: per the (widely cited, originally IBM Systems Sciences Institute) cost curve, "a bug fixed during the implementation phase costs around $80, but when that same bug is found in production, the price tag jumps to $7,600 — nearly a 100x increase," and a production security bug "triggers an all-hands-on-deck emergency where developers, QA testers, security analysts, and Ops engineers all drop what they're doing." This is the central evidentiary basis for shift-left security. [IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/shift-left-catch-security-risks-earlier-across-application-code-infrastructure), [ResearchGate figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/IBM-System-Science-Institute-Relative-Cost-of-Fixing-Defects_fig1_255965523)
4. **Legal requirement → product requirement**: DPIA must be completed "prior to the processing" (GDPR Art. 35), meaning the privacy/legal requirement must enter product requirements before data flows are built, not after — a legally forced instance of shift-left. [GDPR.eu](https://gdpr.eu/data-protection-impact-assessment-template/)
5. **FTO review timing**: best practice is to run FTO "at more advanced stages of a product's development, but ideally before its commercial launch," with a final re-check at design-freeze — meaning IP/legal review is deliberately positioned late relative to security review, creating its own late-arrival risk if the design has already hardened around the year's assumptions. [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/when-is-a-freedom-to-operate-opinion-cost-effective.html)

## Gates and decision criteria

- **DPIA before processing — a legal gate, not a preference**: GDPR Art. 35 requires it whenever processing is "likely to result in a high risk to individuals" (e.g., systematic/extensive profiling, large-scale special-category data, systematic monitoring of public areas), and it must occur before processing begins. Non-compliance draws fines up to €10M / 2% global turnover under Art. 83(4) — this is legally, not organizationally, granted authority to block. [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/article-35-gdpr/), [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/data-protection-impact-assessment/)
- **DPO appointment — legal gate on org design**: mandatory per Art. 37 conditions above; failure to appoint is itself a finable violation independent of any specific incident. [Secure Privacy](https://support.secureprivacy.ai/article/when-is-appointing-a-dpo-mandatory/)
- **Security review sign-off before launch**: an organizational gate modeled on SDL's Release phase — "ensuring the software meets security standards before deployment" — with authority delegated by the organization to the security team, not by statute. [Microsoft SDL](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/securityengineering/sdl/)
- **Penetration test before release**: commonly an organizational/contractual gate (customer contracts or SOC 2/ISO 27001 certification cycles require periodic pen tests as evidence), not itself a statutory requirement in most jurisdictions. [ISMS.online](https://www.isms.online/soc-2/audit-prep/how-to-collect-audit-ready-evidence/)
- **License approval before adding a dependency**: organizational gate to manage copyright/license-compliance risk; enforceable in the sense that violating an OSS license is a legal (copyright) exposure, but the internal *approval process* itself is an organizational control, not a statute.
- **Accessibility conformance before public launch**: for the EU, the **European Accessibility Act (EAA)**, in force since June 28, 2025, is a legal gate — digital products/services sold into the EU must conform to EN 301 549 (incorporating WCAG 2.1 Level AA), enforced by government authorities in each member state (unlike the US ADA's lawsuit-driven enforcement model). This makes EU accessibility conformance a legally, not just organizationally, granted blocking authority, while US ADA compliance is enforced through private litigation risk rather than a pre-launch regulatory checkpoint. [AccessibilityChecker EAA guide](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/guides/eaa-compliance/), [WCAG.com EAA](https://www.wcag.com/compliance/european-accessibility-act/), [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/compliance-overview/european-accessibility-act-eaa/)
- **SBOM at delivery**: legally required for vendors selling software to the US federal government under EO 14028; an organizational choice for purely commercial (non-government) vendors, though customer contracts increasingly mirror the requirement. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-order-14028-improving-nations-cybersecurity/software-security-supply-chains-software-1)
- **Blocking severity thresholds for vulnerabilities**: organizational policy (e.g., "no Critical/High CVSS findings open at launch"); not itself dictated by statute, though it is often the mechanism by which organizations operationalize duty-of-care obligations that *do* have legal weight (e.g., breach-notification exposure).
- **Who has authority to block a launch**: in the surveyed material, only the DPIA/DPO chain and the EU accessibility (EAA) chain carry a statutory can't-launch-without-this character; security review sign-off, pen-test gates, license gates, and vulnerability-severity thresholds are organizationally granted authorities that a company chooses to make binding on itself, typically via a TPM-for-compliance-run process or executive risk-acceptance framework. [unsourced synthesis based on the above sourced gates — no single source states this comparison directly]

## Practice variation and open debates

- **Central security team vs. security champions**: the champions model is explicitly a scaling mechanism — champions "provide visibility of their team's security activities to the application security team" and reduce "the bottleneck of all security decisions flowing through a central security function," but the debate over whether champions genuinely move the needle or just add a title is active in practitioner literature (e.g., "What are security champions, and do they work?"). [AppCheck](https://appcheck-ng.com/what-are-security-champions-and-do-they-work/), [OWASP Developer Guide](https://devguide.owasp.org/en/08-culture-process/02-security-champions/01-security-champions-program/)
- **Shift-left vs. gate-at-the-end**: the IBM cost curve is the standard argument for shift-left, but it is also disputed in the industry as an old/re-derived figure rather than a rigorously reproduced study — worth treating the $80-vs-$7,600 numbers as a widely cited industry heuristic rather than a precise, current empirical result. [ResearchGate figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/IBM-System-Science-Institute-Relative-Cost-of-Fixing-Defects_fig1_255965523)
- **Does threat modeling scale?**: Shostack's four-question framework was deliberately built as "not a methodology but a foundation," explicitly designed to be "flexible such that other threat modeling practices and processes can fit inside" it — an implicit acknowledgment that rigid, formal threat-modeling exercises don't scale to every team/feature, motivating the lighter four-question approach and the Threat Modeling Manifesto's framing of threat modeling as "both art and science." [Shostack](https://shostack.org/blog/four-question-frame/)
- **BSIMM as descriptive vs. prescriptive**: because BSIMM only reports what 111 observed organizations do ("a reflection of current practices"), it cannot tell a team what it *should* do — an open methodological gap relative to SAMM/ASVS, which are explicitly normative. [Secureframe](https://secureframe.com/frameworks-glossary/bsimm), [Black Duck](https://www.blackduck.com/resources/analyst-reports/bsimm.html)
- **SAMM vs. ASVS scope confusion**: practitioner sources explicitly flag that people conflate the two — "SAMM evaluates how well the organization's overall program supports secure software delivery" (org-level maturity) vs. ASVS's per-application control checklist — and organizations sometimes adopt one without realizing they need both. [Resilient Cyber](https://www.resilientcyber.io/p/application-security-maturity-models), [Codific SAMM](https://codific.com/owasp-samm-comprehensive-introduction/)
- **Research-vs-legal-advice boundary in IP**: professional guidance is explicit that internal FTO/prior-art research is not a substitute for a formal legal opinion when infringement risk is material — "When Is a 'Freedom to Operate' Opinion Cost-Effective?" frames this as an economic and risk-based decision on when to engage outside counsel rather than a bright-line rule, i.e., organizations must actively judge when to escalate from research to legal advice. [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/when-is-a-freedom-to-operate-opinion-cost-effective.html)
- **ADA lawsuit-driven vs. EAA regulator-driven enforcement**: the two major accessibility regimes differ fundamentally in enforcement mechanics — US ADA relies on private litigation after the fact, while the EU's EAA is enforced proactively "by government authorities in each member state" — a live debate in accessibility practice about which enforcement model actually drives better pre-launch conformance behavior. [WCAG.com EAA](https://www.wcag.com/compliance/european-accessibility-act/)
